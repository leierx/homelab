# 2026-11-06: loft01 down after the weekly upgrade reboot, CoreDNS rejected its config

## Summary

The weekly `nixos-upgrade` rebooted `loft01` at 02:28 CET. After the boot,
CoreDNS crash-looped: the `coredns-custom` ConfigMap generated by
`nixos/k3s.nix` had a space inside the split-horizon `rewrite` regex, and
CoreDNS refuses to start on a config it can't parse. Without cluster DNS every
app that looks up its database, Keycloak or the Envoy Gateway control plane
failed, and both Gateways stopped serving. Nothing alerted, because
alerting needs DNS too.

The broken config had been in the cluster since 04 Nov 21:02, about 29 hours.
CoreDNS's `reload` plugin rejected it then and kept the old config running, so
it surfaced only when the pod was recreated by the reboot.

## Impact

- From 02:28 CET every public and WireGuard-only service was down: Immich,
  Miniflux, OpenCloud, Grafana, Keycloak (`auth.skinke.net` refused
  connections), Argo CD and Argo Workflows UIs, Alerta.
- `kubectl` through Keycloak was impossible: no Keycloak to log in to, and the
  API server couldn't fetch its discovery document either. Only the host's
  admin kubeconfig through `doas` worked.
- No alerts or mails: Mimir's ruler couldn't resolve
  `alertmanager.monitoring.svc`, and Alertmanager couldn't have resolved Alerta
  anyway. Email wouldn't have worked even with DNS (outbound port 25 is
  blocked, see Recovery).
- Renovate's hourly runs failed. The `dodde` VM, OpenBao and the databases
  kept running. No data loss.

## Root cause

`services.k3s.manifests.<name>.content` is serialised with
`pkgs.formats.yaml_1_2`, whose emitter writes long strings as double-quoted
scalars folded across lines. It split the rewrite line right after an escaped
backslash:

```yaml
split-horizon.override: "bind eth0\nrewrite name regex \\.skinke\\.net(\\..*\\.cluster\\
    .local)?\\.$ edge.envoy-gateway-system.svc.cluster.local. answer auto\n"
```

`\\` is a complete escape (a literal backslash), so the line break after it is
an ordinary fold, which YAML reads as a space. The regex became two arguments,
`\.skinke\.net(\..*\.cluster\` and `.local)?\.$`, and CoreDNS's `rewrite`
rejects the extra argument. The Nix source was correct; only the generated
file was wrong, and nothing looked at the generated file.

The same content had worked for over an hour when it came from a hand-written
YAML file in the `cilium` addon, which is why the move looked like a no-op.

## Why it stayed hidden for 29 hours

- CoreDNS's `reload` keeps the last good config when a new one fails. That is
  the right behaviour for serving, but it turned a hard failure into one log
  line.
- Nothing alerts on CoreDNS reload failures, although CoreDNS is scraped
  (`kube-dns` ServiceMonitor) and its logs are in Loki.
- No restart of CoreDNS happened in between: the 05 Nov k3s restart doesn't
  restart containers. The first restart was the weekly upgrade's reboot at
  night.

## Why it was total

Cluster DNS is a single point of failure for almost everything, including the
paths you would use to notice and fix it: alert delivery (Mimir → Alertmanager
→ Alerta/SMTP), SSO and `kubectl` through Keycloak, and both Gateways. Only
the host's break-glass kubeconfig was left, which is how it was diagnosed.

## Recovery (CET)

- **18:24** 745d372 pushed to `main`: `coredns-custom` is generated with
  `pkgs.formats.json` instead of `content`. JSON is valid YAML and is never
  folded, and the manifest keeps its name, `coredns-custom.yaml`.
  `nixos-rebuild dry-activate` showed that only `systemd-tmpfiles-resetup`
  would change.
- **18:25** `nixos-rebuild switch` from `main` (generation 51). k3s applies the
  new manifest within seconds. The crash-looping CoreDNS pod is deleted and
  the replacement is Ready at 18:25:11. The switch also regenerates
  `/etc/doas.conf`, which removes the temporary rule.
- **18:27–18:31** Keycloak, Immich, Miniflux, Alerta and argo-server come back
  as their backoffs expire. The edge and wg Envoys are Ready at 18:31, and
  `auth.skinke.net` answers again. OIDC `kubectl` works from the workstation.
- **18:35** The last Degraded apps were `alertmanager` and `grafana`: their
  ExternalSecrets had failed to reach OpenBao during the outage and were in
  backoff on a 1 h refresh. A `force-sync` annotation fixes both. Every Argo CD
  app is Synced and Healthy.
- **18:31 onwards** Delivery to Alerta works again. Mimir re-sent what it
  held, among it `SmartStatusFailed`, `ZFSPoolUnhealthy` and `OpenBaoSealed`.
  All three fired through their `absent(...)` branch because no metrics reached
  Mimir during the outage, and all resolved on their own. Every disk reports
  SMART status 1, the pool is ONLINE and OpenBao is unsealed.
- **18:40** Cleanup: the Failed pods left by the two shutdowns are deleted.
  These were 95 `cilium-operator` pods rejected with `NodeShutdown`, 13 pods
  killed by the 04 and 06 Nov shutdowns, 13 failed Renovate retries from the
  outage, and 29 ReplicaSet pods left `Completed` by the shutdown.
  `cilium-operator` piled up because it tolerates
  `node.kubernetes.io/not-ready`: while the node shut down, the scheduler kept
  placing each replacement on it, and the kubelet kept rejecting them. Other
  Deployments don't tolerate the taint and stayed Pending instead.
- **18:48** `alloy-events` restarted. It had been OOMKilled at its 256 Mi limit
  every ~1 h 53 min from 04:25 to 17:34 while it buffered events it couldn't
  push to Loki, and none on the previous boot. It was still at 211 Mi
  (normally ~90 Mi).

The health check afterwards found one older problem: **email alerts have
never been delivered.** Alertmanager mails critical alerts straight to
`mail.protonmail.ch:25`, and Mimir has no successful email notification on
record. The "critical alerts by email" channel in `docs/monitoring.md` didn't
exist even before this outage. Tests at 19:00–19:15:

| Test | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Probe pod on loft01 to port 25 of both Proton MXs, Gmail's MX, and `portquiz.net` (which answers on every port) | All time out. Cilium allows the SYN and sends it out on `eno1`; nothing comes back |
| Same probe to port 587 of `smtp.protonmail.ch` and `smtp.gmail.com`, and to `portquiz.net` on 587 and 2525 | All answer |
| Synthetic critical alert `EmailDeliveryTest` through Alertmanager | Alerta gets it; email fails with `dial tcp 176.119.200.128:25: i/o timeout` |
| Same mail from the workstation, on another ISP, to Proton's MX on port 25 | Accepted (`250 Ok: queued as 4jLnF268S3zNs`), although SPF fails from that IP |

So outbound port 25 is blocked at loft01's uplink (Lyse/Altibox,
`184.81-167-247.customer.lyse.net`) for every destination except Altibox's
own relay, and Proton's side works. The relay (`smtp.altibox.no:25`)
rejects us at `RCPT`: `Client host [81.167.247.184] blocked using
zen.spamhaus.org; Listed by PBL`. On 587 it wants a login. ntfy.sh no longer
sends email anonymously, and Proton's SMTP submission needs a paid plan and
`skinke.net` as a custom domain.

Outage: 02:28 to 18:31 CET, about 16 hours. It took about an hour from the
start of the investigation to recovery. The off-site backup still ran, at 02:47
(`OnBootSec`) and at 09:44, because it only needs the local API.

## Follow-up: pod DNS reworked (CET)

After recovery, the question was why `coredns-custom` lived in Nix at all.
It had two jobs: the split-horizon rewrite, and an upstream server block,
bound to `127.0.0.53` inside the CoreDNS pod, that forced TCP to the host's
resolved. The kubelet's `resolv-conf` pointed CoreDNS at `127.0.0.53`, so
without the ConfigMap CoreDNS forwarded to itself and its `loop` plugin
stopped it. That made the ConfigMap a bootstrap dependency, so c303924 moved
it into Nix, where it was generated and broke.

- **19:50** c520efb and 1c88f7f try to drop the upstream block: the kubelet
  points CoreDNS straight at resolved on `192.168.2.50`, which now listens on
  UDP too, and the k3s manifest goes. k3s leaves the ConfigMap in place when
  its manifest file disappears (`pkg/deploy/controller.go` only deletes
  disabled manifests).
- **19:58:37** CoreDNS restarted on that config. External lookups from pods
  time out (cluster names and the rewrite still work), from 19:58:44 to
  20:00:13. resolved receives and answers the queries, but for extra stub
  listeners it sends UDP replies with `IP_PKTINFO` pinned to the ingress
  interface (`resolved-dns-stub.c`). Cilium delivers pod→host traffic in on
  `cilium_net`, while the route back to pods is `cilium_host`, so the replies
  never arrive. TCP is unaffected, which is why the old block forced TCP.
- **20:00** Mitigated without root: CoreDNS's Deployment gets
  `dnsPolicy: None` with nameserver `127.0.0.53`, so it uses the old TCP
  block, which is still in the ConfigMap.
- **20:05** Tested in a scratch pod on the same CoreDNS 1.14.6 image: an
  upstream block on `127.0.0.53` that speaks DoT to Quad9. The main block
  can't stay on the wildcard ("zone overlap" with `127.0.0.53`), and
  `bind eth0` fails on every first start because eth0's IPv6 link-local
  address isn't bindable yet. In production this showed up as one restart
  per new CoreDNS pod. `bind 0.0.0.0` works: lookups, the rewrite, DNSSEC
  failures (`SERVFAIL`) and `NXDOMAIN` all behave as expected, over DoT only.
- **20:10** 8642ca2 lets CoreDNS reach world:853, and 8548612 puts
  `coredns-custom` back in the `cilium` addon as a plain YAML file
  (`bind 0.0.0.0`, DoT to Quad9). CoreDNS reloads it live; upstream traffic
  goes to `9.9.9.9:853`.
- **20:16** 9f9f340 switched (the first switch built a cached `main`, so the
  rev was pinned): the kubelet is back on `127.0.0.53`, and resolved no longer
  listens on `192.168.2.50`. The temporary Deployment patch is reverted, so
  CoreDNS is k3s's stock Deployment again. The stale k3s `Addon` and k3s's
  tracking metadata on the ConfigMap are deleted. A new CoreDNS pod starts
  with zero restarts.
- **20:18** 247811a drops CoreDNS's egress to host:53 and the host's ingress
  rule for it, and bootstrap step 1 applies `coredns-custom` again
  (`fleet/README.md`).

Now resolved only serves WireGuard peers (`10.0.0.1`) and the host itself.
Pods use CoreDNS, which talks DoT to Quad9 directly, and Quad9 validates
DNSSEC.
- **20:52** 2778e6c and 21bd669 move `coredns-custom` back into
  `nixos/k3s.nix`, as a literal YAML string, so k3s applies it at boot and
  nothing generates it. `Prune=false` kept Argo CD from deleting it on the way
  out.

## Lessons

- A serialisation step is part of the change. The Nix source was correct and
  reviewed, but nobody looked at the YAML k3s actually applied. Read the
  generated artifact (`nix build` it and `cat` it) when moving config between
  formats or generators.
- A reloader that keeps the last good config hides failures until the next
  restart. Every such reload needs a metric and an alert, or the next restart
  needs to happen while someone is watching.
- The weekly unattended reboot runs at night, so anything that only breaks on
  restart surfaces then, with nobody around. Fine for real upgrades, but alerts
  have to reach someone when the cluster itself is down, which today they
  can't.
- Cluster DNS is a dependency of the tools for noticing and fixing a DNS outage
  too (Keycloak, `kubectl` over OIDC, Alertmanager, both Gateways). The host's
  `doas` kubeconfig was the only way in, as `docs/README.md` intends.
- Test a DNS path change in a scratch pod before it reaches CoreDNS. The
  resolved/Cilium UDP problem cost 90 seconds of pod DNS. The same test, run
  first, found the `bind` constraints before production did.
- Pin `nixos-rebuild --flake` to a rev (or `--refresh`) right after a push.
  Nix caches the `ref=main` lookup, so a quick second switch can silently build
  the previous commit.
